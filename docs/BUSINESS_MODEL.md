# Moneliora: service-first validation model

## Decision

Use free scenario tools to demonstrate utility, then validate a narrowly scoped, one-time educational session. Do not launch a paid subscription solely because subscription integrations exist in the repository.

This is a proposed model, not a validated business, promised income, or proof that people will buy. Two days is a reasonable window for an initial demand experiment, not a reliable revenue deadline.

## Customer, problem and offer

| Element | First experiment |
| --- | --- |
| Customer hypothesis | Adults who can enter their figures but need help understanding a cash-flow or savings scenario |
| Problem | They struggle to turn calculator outputs into a clear next action |
| Free proof | Cash-flow planner, savings scenarios, CSV export and printable action worksheet |
| Paid hypothesis | A 45-minute walkthrough of the tools plus a completed next-step worksheet |
| Price hypothesis | €49; not yet an advertised final price or an enabled checkout |
| Second segment | Solo founders modelling one product/service; 60-minute business-numbers session at a €99 price hypothesis |
| Delivery boundary | Explain tool mechanics and assumptions; no securities recommendations, investment suitability, account access, tax/legal advice or promised results |
| First channel hypothesis | Existing relevant contacts and communities that permit sharing educational tools; no automated unsolicited outreach |
| Retention hypothesis | Follow-up educational needs identified in interviews; recurring pricing only after evidence of repeat value |

A price hypothesis should not be treated as a tax-inclusive offer until the operator has established applicable tax treatment, actual scope and customer terms. The website labels hypotheses and does not accept payment for these sessions.

## Why this model first

| Model | Two-day constraint | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Display advertising | Requires audience scale; distracts from the planning task | Defer |
| Investment affiliate links | Introduces conflicts and disclosure responsibilities before trust is established | Defer |
| Consumer subscription | Requires repeat usage, support and a validated ongoing benefit | Validate later |
| One-time educational guidance | Bounded scope, observable delivery cost, direct customer feedback | Test first |

These comparisons are implementation judgments, not measured conversion or revenue evidence.

## Economics: include the founder's time

The launch lab separates gross sales, net revenue, contribution and the result after fixed costs. It limits modelled sales to delivery capacity and accounts for VAT assumptions, refunds, nonrefunded processing fees, delivery cost, delivery time and acquisition time.

The default *hypothetical* example has 100 qualified visits, 10% inquiry conversion and 20% close conversion: two modelled sales. At €49, 5% assumed refunds, 3% + €0.30 fees, €2 delivery cost, one delivery hour valued at €25, contribution is €17.78 per order. Two acquisition hours valued at €25 and €20 other costs produce a **€34.44 loss**, despite €98 gross sales. VAT is assumed zero in that example, not asserted to be legally applicable.

That loss is useful evidence about assumptions: test price, scope and customer demand before buying traffic. Do not hide unpaid founder time or report gross sales as profit. Replace every assumption with observed values before making spending decisions.

## 48-hour experiment

| Time | Work | Concrete output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hours 0–4 | Pick one customer/problem; rehearse the worksheet with synthetic data | One scope and realistic delivery-time estimate |
| Hours 4–8 | Conduct five permission-based customer conversations | Anonymised problem/alternative/willingness-to-pay observations |
| Hours 8–12 | Revise the offer and pricing model | Updated assumptions, explicit continue/change/stop decision |
| Hours 12–24 | Prepare operator details, actual availability, privacy/terms and request handling | Operator-ready request configuration; no fabricated availability |
| Hours 24–36 | Share a free tool through one permitted channel; handle genuine inquiries | Measured visits/inquiries from available channel records; no invented analytics |
| Hours 36–48 | If separately agreed, deliver a pilot session and review the result | Worksheet, actual delivery time and a truthful record of any payment outside this app |

No outreach, scheduling or payment is executed by this plan. If prerequisites are unavailable, keep the pilot closed and use the period for interviews and rehearsal.

## Measures and stop rules

- A visit, interest request, booking, payment and delivered session are different events. Never label an interest request as revenue.
- Track qualified conversations, observed willingness to pay, agreed scope, delivery time and actual contribution. Protect customer information separately.
- Change the offer if interviews do not reveal a recurring problem or customers will not accept a price above delivery cost.
- Do not spend on acquisition when contribution is nonpositive, or when required sales exceed available capacity.
- Do not claim validated demand from hypothetical funnel percentages or the launch checklist.
